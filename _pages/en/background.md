My academic background lies at the intersection of social history, economic history, and gender history.

After completing two years of French literary preparatory classes (_Classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles_, CPGE A/L), specialising in History and Geography at Lycée Gabriel Guist’hau in Nantes, I continued my studies at Université Paris Nanterre, where I obtained a joint degree in History and English with honours.

I subsequently joined the Master’s programme in History and the Social Sciences of Politics at Université Paris Nanterre, under the supervision of Patrice Baubeau and Laurence Croq. This degree, completed with honours, enabled me to develop an approach combining economic and social history, quantitative analysis, and the study of individual practices.

My Master’s dissertation, entitled _“Ma tante a un secret : elle préfère les diamants de la folie aux guenilles de la misère. Les pratiques irrégulières du prêt sur gage dans le registre d’engagements secrets du Mont-de-Piété de Paris à la fin du XIXe siècle”_, examined clandestine and irregular pawning practices through a register of confidential pledges from the Paris Mont-de-Piété.

This research provided the starting point for my current work on the relationships between gender, credit, reputation, and economic practices in nineteenth-century France.

My second-year Master’s dissertation received a research grant from the Historical Mission of the Banque de France. I also completed training in Geographic Information Systems (GIS) at the EHESS, allowing me to broaden my methodological toolkit and develop a spatial approach to historical sources.

This work now forms the basis of a doctoral project currently being prepared for the 2027–2028 academic year, focusing on confidential pledges at the Paris Mont-de-Piété between 1866 and 1918. The project examines how individuals mobilised their possessions to obtain liquidity while protecting their reputation, with particular attention to the influence of gender on credit practices.

The corpus combines serial, quantitative, and prosopographical approaches with gender history and the history of credit institutions.

I teach History, Geography, and Civic and Moral Education at lower-secondary level.

My teaching places particular emphasis on document analysis, mapping, inquiry-based learning, collaborative work, and the gradual development of student autonomy. I seek to combine knowledge acquisition, disciplinary methods, and the development of critical thinking.

Particular attention is paid to the analysis of images and maps, media and information literacy, and the understanding of contemporary issues at different geographical scales.

I also develop projects that enable students to produce knowledge and representations themselves through mapping competitions, research activities, visual productions, debates, source analysis, and interdisciplinary approaches.

One of the broader aims of my teaching is to build bridges between historical and geographical research and school learning by introducing students to documents, methods, and questions derived from contemporary scholarship.

### Projects

My teaching and community engagement are strongly project-based, with particular emphasis on cartography, inquiry, collaborative work, and equality.

#### Concours Carto – Imaginary Mapping and Current Affairs Mapping

I prepare and support my students for a national cartography competition through two complementary formats: imaginary mapping with Year 7 students (_5e_) and current affairs mapping with Year 8 students (_4e_).

The project develops students’ ability to read and prioritise information, construct a map legend, use cartographic language, and visually represent a territory or a contemporary issue. It also gives considerable space to creativity, autonomy, and reflection on the choices involved in geographical representation.

#### Eco-Camp

I contribute to an educational project focusing on environmental issues and sustainable development through an eco-camp organised by my school.

The project provides students with an opportunity to approach questions relating to resources, ecological transition, and sustainable practices through practical experiences combining awareness-raising, observation, and active participation.

#### Mapping the School

This project turns the students’ own school into a fieldwork site.

Year 7 students conduct observations and collect data in order to identify potential inequalities in the ways different areas of the school are used and appropriated. Year 8 students then develop this inquiry further through a cartographic project.

The aim is to show students that geographical tools can be applied to familiar environments and can help them investigate spatial practices, inequalities, perceptions, and different forms of appropriation within everyday spaces.

#### Equality and Gender Inclusion at the Club Alpin Français de Chambéry

I am also involved in the gender equality and inclusion committee of the Club Alpin Français de Chambéry, where I contribute to initiatives designed to promote women’s participation and greater gender inclusion in mountain sports.

I founded a climbing group based on chosen gender-mixed participation, creating a space specifically designed to encourage women’s participation in climbing. Among the initiatives I have developed are informal women climbers’ meet-ups, organised as opportunities to climb, meet one another, and exchange experiences.

Together with other club volunteers, I am also currently working on an analysis of gender-related statistics within the club, with the aim of documenting differences in participation, sporting practices, and potential inequalities across activities.

This work brings together community engagement, data production, and a broader reflection on the social mechanisms that can facilitate or restrict access to mountain sports.
